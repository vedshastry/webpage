About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Economics at the University of California, Santa Cruz (UCSC). My research interests lie in environmental, development, and applied microeconomics. I look to understand how climate and technological change shape economic activity over the long run.

My current work studies warming temperatures and structural change in India. I am also interested in how growing resource demands of digital/AI infrastructure interact with the water, energy and food security nexus.

Prior to grad school, I provided research assistance on several projects in development economics and public policy spanning irrigation, agricultural markets, impact evaluations and home-grown school feeding with the World Bank and the Indian School of Business.

Beyond economics, I'm enthusiastic about travel, languages, playing music, chess, (e)biking, and cooking a meal every Sunday.

Working Papers

  • Night and Day: Diurnal Warming and Structural Transformation in India

    [arXiv Paper] [Supplemental/Online Appendix]
    This paper finds diverging partial effects of diurnal warming -- higher nighttime and daytime temperatures -- on agricultural wage-labour shares from decadal Indian Censuses (1981--2011). Though both margins contract grain output and cultivated area, only higher maxima raise harvest prices locally -- consistent with a model where warmer nights shock land but warmer days shock land and labour productivity. Warming nights shift seasonal workers and self-cultivators into agricultural labour; warming days push labour to the seasonal margin. Long differences show the labour divergence is rural. In towns, both margins depress non-agricultural worker shares.
